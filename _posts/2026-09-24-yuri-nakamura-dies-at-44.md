Actress Yuri Nakamura (中村ゆり, real name 中村友理) died of rectal cancer on September 1, 2026, at age 44. Her agency, Alpha Agency, announced the death on September 14. She died surrounded by her family and close friends, and a private funeral was held with relatives and colleagues.

For twenty years she was one of those actors viewers recognized on sight: a doctor's colleague, a detective's partner, a wife quietly holding a household together. Her performances were gentle and precise, and she never seemed to be showing off. It is hard to accept that there will be no new ones.

## A Fight She Kept to Herself

According to her agency, Nakamura was first diagnosed with cancer 13 years ago. She went into remission and kept working, telling almost no one so that the people she worked with would not worry. In January 2025 the cancer returned. Surgery went well, but later tests showed it had spread.

In July 2026 she withdrew from the NHK Drama 10 series *Dangerous* (デンジャラス), scheduled for January 2027, and publicly gave a bowel obstruction as the reason. In August, while she was still recovering and working toward a return, the obstruction came back and doctors told her she had very little time left. The agency said she kept "a beautiful, gentle smile" to the end.

After her death, many people recalled her 2019 Nippon Life commercial, in which she played a seriously ill mother. Knowing what she was living with at the time makes it very hard to watch now.

## From Pop Duo to Actress

Nakamura was born on March 15, 1982, in Neyagawa, Osaka, into a Zainichi Korean family, and held South Korean nationality throughout her life.

| Year | Milestone |
|---|---|
| 1996 | Passed an audition on TV Tokyo's *ASAYAN* and formed the pop duo YURIMARI with her childhood friend MARI |
| 1998 | YURIMARI debuted, releasing six singles and one album before disbanding in 1999 |
| 2003 | Returned after a three-year break, now as an actress |
| 2007 | Played the lead, Kyonja Ri, in Kazuyuki Izutsu's *Pacchigi! Love & Peace*, winning the National Film Association (全国映連賞) Best Actress award and the Osaka Cinema Festival Newcomer Award |
| 2013 | Appeared in Hirokazu Kore-eda's *Like Father, Like Son*; joined Alpha Agency, where she remained for the rest of her career |
| 2020 | Took her first TV lead at 37, sharing top billing with Kodai Asaka in *Konya wa Ko no Ji de* |
| 2023 | Won Best Supporting Actress at the Takasaki Film Festival for *Ichiko* |
| 2025 | Reached a worldwide audience in Netflix's *Romantics Anonymous* |

Her other TV work includes *Garasu no Ashi* (2015), *Nusumareta Kao* (WOWOW, 2019), *Omoshiro Nankyoku Ryōrinin* (2019), *Watashitachi wa Dōka Shiteiru* (2020) and *Tokkai* (2021).

On the big screen, she appeared in *Sakuran* (2007), *Parade* (2010), *Hayabusa: Harukanaru Kikan* (2012), *After the Storm* (2016), *The 8-Year Engagement* (2017), *Fukushima 50* (2020), *Arc* (2021) and *Ichiko* (2023), among many others.

## Tributes

Colleagues responded with shock and grief. Their comments, reported in Japanese media and paraphrased here in English:

- **MARI**, her YURIMARI partner and friend of 35 years: she never complained, was full of compassion, and was kind, strong and beautiful to everyone.
- **Ryohei Suzuki**, who met her at acting school 20 years ago: strong yet soft, dignified and beautiful, "like a lily."
- **Jun Kaname**, who recently played her husband: he resents how cruel illness is.
- **Jun Fubuki**: quiet and kind, a wonderful actress.

Koji Seto, Hideo Nakano, director Shunji Iwai, kabuki actor Koshiro Matsumoto and singer Shikao Suga also paid tribute. Suga said he last contacted her in July, when she stepped away to rest.

## Thank You, Yuri

Her name means "lily," and Ryohei Suzuki reached for the same flower when he described her. She kept working for thirteen years knowing she was ill, and stayed kind to the people around her. Her performances are still there for anyone who wants to watch them again.

May she rest in peace. 心よりご冥福をお祈りいたします。
