Fuji TV's medical drama *Kyumei Byoto 24-ji* (救命病棟24時, "Emergency Room 24 Hours") comes back on **October 12, 2026**, its first new season in about 13 years. It is the sixth series, and the first to air in Fuji's prestigious Monday 9 p.m. slot, with a 15-minute extended first episode. Yosuke Eguchi and Nanako Matsushima, the show's two leads, reunite on screen for the first time in roughly 16 years.

If you have never seen the show, here is what it is and why Japanese viewers care.

## What Is *Kyumei Byoto 24-ji*?

The series follows the emergency center of a large hospital. Its centerpiece is the relationship between **Issei Shindo** (Eguchi), a brilliant and demanding emergency physician, and **Kaede Kojima** (Matsushima), who first appears as a nervous new resident under his supervision. Each season picks a real social or medical problem in Japan and builds its stories around it. From 1999 on, the show ran in Fuji TV's Tuesday 9 p.m. slot and regularly averaged close to 20% viewership.

Season 2 is the outlier: Matsushima was not in the main cast, and Eguchi carried it with Taiko Matsuyuki and Hideaki Ito. Otherwise the arc is clear. Kaede grows from trainee to director, while Shindo stays the cool, exacting mentor.

**Where to start:** Season 1 (1999) is the origin story and worth watching first. If you'd rather skip ahead, the new season starts with the two characters as veterans, so you can follow it without having seen the earlier ones.

## Season 6: What We Know

- **Premiere:** Monday, October 12, 2026, 9 p.m. on Fuji TV
- **Leads:** Yosuke Eguchi (Shindo) and Nanako Matsushima (Kaede)
- **Supporting cast:** Daisei Kido, Toshihisa Hagiwara, Kiyoe Shiraishi, Mayu Yokota
- **Setting:** The emergency center of Tokyo Metropolitan Third Hospital, the same location as Season 1
- **Music:** Masahiro Tokuda, the composer of the series' well-known opening theme

## In Their Own Words: The Eguchi and Matsushima Interview

In a newly released interview, the two leads talk about coming back. The quotes below are my translations from the Japanese.

**On returning after so long.** Eguchi says the long gap feels *fresh* rather than nostalgic: "I'm looking at it more with anticipation for what kind of drama it will be." Matsushima says that each season has its own theme and she felt excited about "what they'd take up this time." She adds that they had bumped into each other privately a few times, so it did not feel like meeting after 16 years.

**A relationship that has changed.** Matsushima explains that Kaede started as a trainee who *bumped into* Shindo, and that six series later she has grown alongside the character, who is now becoming head of the department. "Now we're both veterans, so Kaede has more emotional room. I'm thinking of engaging Dr. Shindo in a more *relaxed* way." Eguchi, who hears this for the first time in the interview, reacts with "You're thinking that?" and admits they have not started filming. He notes that Shindo has always been cool and strict, so he is curious how relaxed the two can get after all these years.

**Why the show still matters.** Eguchi reflects that when the series began, an emergency-room drama felt special, about restoring life on the spot. Now there are many medical dramas. He says each country's medical drama reflects its own healthcare system, but the core theme stays the same: *how to face the patient and how to face life.* That is why the show can still bring "emotion and a sense of hope."

**Modern medicine and today's problems.** Matsushima says that medical equipment has become so advanced over the last 10 to 20 years that even she and Eguchi were surprised, and she wants to show viewers what a real emergency room looks like today. Eguchi says the show will again take in issues such as workplace harassment and shift schedules, along with problems specific to Japan such as end-of-life care and disaster victims, with a wide range of patients in the stories.

Eguchi calls this "the new *Kyumei* of 2026," made with a largely different cast and staff, and hopes it will "show a light of emotion and hope."

## Why It Matters

The Monday 9 p.m. slot is Fuji's flagship "Getsu-9" drama block, and the network is betting on a familiar title in a crowded market. Some Japanese commentary has questioned Fuji leaning on past hits. Yet the show's strength was never novelty. It is the pairing of Eguchi and Matsushima and an honest look at real medical problems in Japan. If the new season keeps to that, it could bring back longtime viewers and win over new ones.
